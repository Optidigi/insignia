## M1 dependency and proposed sequencing decision

[Plan §15](https://github.com/Optidigi/insignia/blob/662a78cd27507d8a2f1eaa976f1c644c93edd1be/docs/architecture/implementation-plan.md#L791-L815) says M0 executes G1–G8 and yields narrow contracts before M1. It also assigns integrated storefront/context cases to M7, purchase facts to M8, the durable three-plan billing implementation and tests to M9, and gate reruns on the release candidate to M10. In particular, **G8 is named as an M9 prerequisite while M9 builds the durable facts and delivery that G8 itself requires**. G6 publisher activation also depends on production ports introduced in M3/M4. These are sequencing conflicts, not grounds to drop criteria.

**Under the unamended plan, every open material G1–G8 criterion above blocks declaring M0 complete and starting M1.** Recommend a principal-reviewed *narrow sequencing amendment*: define M0 exit as accepted platform feasibility and stable, measured cart/auth/billing interface contracts from minimal spikes; permit M1's workspace only after those named foundations are accepted. Keep each still-open criterion explicitly assigned and unpassed: G1–G6 integrated closure before M7, G7 before M5, provider feasibility before billing implementation, full G8 lifecycle with M8/M9, and all G1–G8 release-candidate repeats before M10 exit/launch. Amend the circular “G8 before M9” wording to require the M0 provider contract proof before M9 and full G8 acceptance at M9 completion. This report **does not apply** that amendment. Actual commercial plan values/features remain owner decisions before paid release; production DB/outbox, hosted endpoint, release deployment, retention/restore, review and merchant install are later implementation/release obligations, not facts proved by the spikes.
