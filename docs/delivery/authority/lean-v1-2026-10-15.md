# Insignia — approved Lean V1

Status: owner-approved product and delivery scope from this conversation. This is a prospective amendment to the broader Insignia baseline, not evidence that the release is already implemented or accepted. No repository, provider, billing or deployment changes were performed when preparing this handoff.

## 1. Product and complete journey

Insignia is a public Shopify product-customization app. Merchants configure decoration choices and publish products. Buyers choose artwork, placements, decoration sizes and real garment variants/quantities, review exact prices and check out. Merchants obtain trustworthy purchase records, historical previews and the original production artwork.

The complete core journey is:

Merchant authenticates → configures and publishes → buyer uploads artwork and customizes → reviews and accepts the price → actual Shopify cart and permitted test checkout → worker independently reconciles the order/payment → durable purchase and first-paid facts → authenticated merchant retrieves the correct original artwork and historical preview.

Preserve the working implementation and reuse valid evidence for unchanged behavior. The revised journey and its changed boundaries still need qualification; earlier successful tests are not proof of the new scope.

## 3. Pricing example and invariant

Illustration only: small front print €3 per garment, large back print €6 per garment, garment base €20. Ten garments with both placements have €290 pre-discount merchandise value. There is no setup fee, tier or currency conversion.

For design group g and variant v:

`total = sum(g, v) quantity[g,v] × (ShopifyBase[g,v] + sum(selected placement decoration prices[g]))`

Use exact existing money handling. Each physical garment contributes its base once and each selected placement surcharge once. Shopify discount/tax/shipping processing does not become a second local price engine. Configuration changes never rewrite an accepted offer or purchased history.

## 4. Prospective enforcement and billing transition

Use the existing pricing engine and narrow new authoring/issuance inputs instead of replacing it. Enforce Lean V1 at applicable backend boundaries as well as the editor. Existing advanced configurations remain readable; they must not remain accidentally eligible for new Lean offers. Any conversion requires an explicit new draft/revision, not mutation of a historical revision.

Preserve old accepted offers under their original terms/expiry and historical readers required for purchase reconciliation and retention. New offers and renewals must meet the new scope; an incompatible old configuration requires a Lean replacement proposal and buyer review. No retrospective zeroing of prices or rewriting of test receipts.

Recurring-only billing requires deliberate provider-contract recognition: do not assume disabling usage dispatch makes a two-item flat-plus-meter entitlement reader correct. Verify the actual approved flat contract and authoritative trial/cancellation/access behavior. Retain purchase/payment records for product correctness. Do not emit usage or back-bill the flat-plan period later.

Inspect any existing financial intent, in-flight operation or actual merchant obligation before a billing transition. Preserve history and reconcile genuine obligations; do not blindly delete queues or retry an uncertain mutation. Provider plan creation/approval/switching, live charges, new resources and rollout still require actual scoped authority.

## 5. Deferred and excluded work

Deferred: layered pricing options, setup fees, quantity tiers, FX/multi-currency overrides, usage billing, separate Plus implementation, dedicated product-page accelerated flows, broad third-party interoperability, and unsupported capacity/context expansion. They are unavailable/not advertised and are no longer launch blockers. Their safe exclusion still needs testing.

Still excluded: legacy data migration/import, RFQ, production-management/fulfillment engine, B2B negotiated pricing, POS/headless integrations, subscription-product customization, order-edit repricing and speculative multi-plan features. Shopify remains the authority for its payment, fulfillment, refund and inventory operations.

Do not delete already-working historical code or tests merely to reduce line counts. Retire executable paths from routine use when no supported runtime, recovery or retained-record contract depends on them.

## 6. Lean verification and delivery

One local engineering lead owns implementation, tools/skills/connections, proportionate independent review, normal integration, permitted development deployment and runtime qualification. There is no principal or routine owner milestone gate. The owner retains material new product/risk decisions, missing resources, human-only actions and rollout authority.

Use meaningful behavior tests, independent expectations and relevant regressions. Keep actual PostgreSQL/role/process tests and TS/Rust/Function contracts where they establish different boundaries. Unsupported features need rejection/exclusion checks, not completion of their old successful-behavior matrix. No arbitrary coverage percentage, test-count, test-LOC or PR quota.

Ordinary behavior changes receive one focused independent review; material money/authentication/privacy/concurrency/release changes receive appropriate correctness and security coverage. A second context is justified by a distinct need, not ritual. Corrections receive impact review; ordinary prose/status/read-only probe corrections do not automatically receive delegated review or behavioral tests.

Consolidate equivalent repeated static/full database checks without losing required check contexts, production-role/process/Function coverage or artifact provenance. Keep the existing narrow documentation route. Select historical controls by actual dependencies. Do not build a generalized test-selection or attestation platform to save small execution costs.

Separate stable reviewed deployment identity from appropriately fresh installation/permission/subscription/Function observations. Simplify the current release-verification implementation while preserving independent authority, real expiry, invalidation and historical facts. Do not remove safety checks by relabeling deployment configuration as verified evidence.

## 7. Completion boundary

A release-qualified Lean V1 demonstrates:

- The complete native merchant → customization → purchase → original-artwork journey under Lean pricing, plus historical preview integrity and permitted artwork replacement/locking.
- Exact totals; variant/quantity correctness; ordinary supported discounts; required-product enforcement; safe rejection and repair for altered/incomplete/expired offers, unsupported contexts and lost responses; important duplicate/restart/concurrency behavior.
- Corresponding legacy storefront visuals/interactions on representative supported desktop/mobile states, and a usable Lean admin including flat-plan/trial management.
- Qualified recurring-only provider contract, trial/cancellation/entitlement behavior, without usage dispatch or retrospective charges.
- Safe private upload/processing/access; required privacy handling and real retention/cleanup; preserved existing expiry and credential-rotation obligations.
- Reproducible Docker/Compose/Traefik operation, documented security prerequisites, readiness, stop/restart, migration compatibility, backup/restore with erasure handling, rollback and actionable monitoring.
- Applicable Shopify/public-app requirements and owner-authorized rollout. Development evidence is not distribution approval.

Revise the existing gate register against this scope: retain applicable criteria, mark removed capabilities deferred/not shipping rather than PASS, and replace hybrid-billing acceptance with recurring-only acceptance. Do not require the old full product matrix as an unchanged launch gate.
