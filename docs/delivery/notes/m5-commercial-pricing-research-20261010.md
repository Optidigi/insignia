# Insignia commercial pricing research — 10 October 2026

Research date: **2026-10-10 UTC**. W10 worktree HEAD when researched: `2f7aa2bfb304a80ac08a2965ed20c63e4c85fe8e`. Public primary-source research only: official Shopify App Store listings and each vendor's own pricing/help pages. No Shopify account, subscription, meter, policy, app version, store, host or deployment operation was performed. This note is the sole researcher-owned repository change.

**Recommendation for owner consideration:** one paid plan containing all released, in-scope features at a **USD $49.99 monthly base** is commercially credible. Evaluate **100 included qualifying paid customized orders and a small fixed fee**, with **$0.20 as a comparison candidate**, before choosing the usage price; also evaluate a smaller fee or a commercial monthly charge ceiling. Retain a **14-day live trial with subscription and usage waived** as the compatible baseline while the meaning of “demo/trial” is resolved. These are recommendations, not approved commercial values or configured provider state. Neither costs nor native merchant value currently justify selecting a usage fee automatically; $0.30/$0.50 need a stronger case at volume.

The base price fits the closest usage-based competitors. The fixed fee per actual paid order is simpler than charging for each personalized item or design and avoids a percentage penalty on large garment orders. However, substantial flat-price competition exists: Insignia cannot claim to be the cheapest customizer at volume. Profitability and merchants' willingness to pay remain unmeasured.

## Fixed fee versus a percentage

Prefer a fixed per-order amount. A **hypothetical** 0.5% fee on customized merchandise would cost $0.15 on $30, $0.30 on $60, $0.50 on $100 and $5 on a $1,000 bulk order; 1% doubles those amounts. A $0.20 fixed charge equals 0.5% at $40 or 1% at $20. These examples are arithmetic, not recommended percentage terms. The base subscription would be additional.

Percentage pricing would require a new owner decision defining its economic base: customized merchandise versus the customization surcharge alone, discounts, tax/shipping exclusion, FX, zero-price transactions and any cap. It also changes the currently recorded one-unit paid-order meter economics. A revenue percentage can charge much more for high-value/bulk orders even when merchant value and compute cost do not rise proportionally. The percentage competitors have defined their own product-sales bases; that does not justify charging on every merchant order. [Kickflip fee basis](https://help.gokickflip.com/en/articles/4690175-how-does-our-pricing-work), [Zakeke fee basis](https://zakeke.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360016160833-How-is-the-transaction-fee-calculated).

Fixed order pricing also has a cost risk: one unusually large order or expensive artwork workload generates the same fee as a small order. Before paid release, measure rendering/storage/egress, support and operational work across actual order sizes, plus base per-store costs. The proposal yields total app revenue of $49.99, $129.99 and $429.99 in the three scenarios; this is revenue before costs, taxes and any applicable platform share. No margin target or cost sufficiency has been established. Preserve supported technical capacity and retention boundaries even with “all features” included; do not promise unlimited capacity or competitor-style multi-store pooling without a separate scope decision.

## Trial/demo recommendation and release ownership

Recommend the same paid plan's **14-day live trial from authoritative activation**, all released in-scope features usable, with **both subscription and qualifying order usage waived** and no retrospective billing. This preserves the existing trial direction and lets a merchant prove configuration, preview and a live workflow. Market evidence supports the duration: inspected trials range from **9 to 21 days**, with Teeinblue/Kickflip/Zakeke at 14 days. Customily and Zakeke expressly waive transaction charges during trial. [Customily](https://www.customily.com/pricing), [Teeinblue](https://apps.shopify.com/teeinblue), [Kickflip](https://apps.shopify.com/mycustomizer-1), [Zakeke](https://zakeke.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360021628279-How-does-the-Free-Trial-work), [Inkybay](https://apps.shopify.com/productsdesigner), [Zepto](https://apps.shopify.com/product-personalizer).

Interpret “demo/trial plan” as that timed trial for now, pending an explicit owner choice. A permanent sample-data demo, development-store free entitlement or separate free live-selling tier would each need clear scope and lifecycle rules; competitor free development-store plans do not establish any of these for Insignia. Do not turn demo preview into a bypass for inactive entitlements or required-product enforcement. The existing inactive-entitlement and historical-access rules remain recorded in the [decision ledger](../../architecture/decision-ledger.md).

**Ownership:** the owner decides the final single-plan commercial structure, currency, recurring amount, fixed fee, included allowance, per-installation versus multi-store scope, and whether demo means the timed trial or an additional capability. The local orchestrator owns reconciling the ledger/plan and reviewing/integrating this note under the [operating model](../operating-model.md); the researcher owns only this note. Billing implementation/native qualification belongs to the corresponding authorized **M9/G8** work and paid-release checks, not this M5 research slice. Principal milestone acceptance and external provider/resource permissions remain separate. No recommendation here approves billing, creates real configuration, accepts M5/M9/G8, or grants merchant rollout.
