# Natural translation review

4 November 2026. Research and workflow recommendation only. No application, catalog, language coverage, dependency or skill installation changes. This complements the [localization plan](localization-implementation-plan-2026-11-02.md) and [wording defaults implementation](translation-wording-defaults-2026-11-04.md).

## Recommendation

Make ordinary, familiar wording an explicit translation requirement, then review meaning and naturalness separately. A translation can be grammatical and still sound strange. Use AI to draft and flag possible problems, source-backed terminology to resolve choices, and a qualified target-language reviewer for linguistic acceptance. A second AI pass is another automated review, not evidence that a native speaker approved the language.

English is currently the only bundled language. The Dutch authoring target and passing Arabic/plural engineering cases do not establish reviewed Dutch or Arabic defaults. Sparse merchant overrides also need a separate status from the shipped baseline. Restoring defaults restores the applicable catalog wording; it does not validate that wording's linguistic quality.

## Proposed review workflow for Insignia

These are project recommendations derived from the sources above, not claims that a standard mandates this exact process.

1. Prepare a small locale brief. State the intended region and audience, form of address, sentence case and the rule to prefer familiar everyday wording. Preserve exact commercial meaning and necessary decoration terminology. A shorter word is not an improvement if it blurs garment size versus decoration size, Save versus Publish, or removing an override versus deleting content.
2. Agree a glossary before translating a workflow. Record the concept, English term, preferred target term, rejected alternatives with reasons and source evidence. Use Shopify's localized product/help wording for Shopify concepts, the selected locale guide for language conventions, and the product definition for Insignia-specific concepts. When sources disagree, record the decision rather than mixing terms. Do not force one target word onto unrelated meanings of the same English word.
3. Give the translator a review packet with each message key, complete source, UI role, triggering state, related messages, screenshot or route, and representative interpolation values. Show the actual consequence for restore, discard, publish and recovery actions. Make visible text and accessible text part of the packet.
4. Review the draft in two passes. First compare source and target for meaning, omissions, additions, terminology and variables. Then read the target as a user without relying on English sentence structure. Flag literal constructions, uncommon synonyms, inconsistent address and phrases a local user would not normally use for that task. AI can identify candidates, but every suggested correction needs context and a reason.
5. Ask a qualified target-language reviewer to inspect complete workflows in the running application. Use the existing desktop/phone states and recovery scenarios. Include labels, tooltips, validation, confirmations and generated human-readable outputs. A reviewer must understand the source meaning or work with a bilingual reviewer; native fluency by itself does not establish domain or editing competence.
6. Resolve findings and retain the accepted wording. Record message key, source, target, context, category, severity, suggested correction, reason/source, reviewer and disposition. Separate an actual defect from an acceptable stylistic preference. Recheck edited phrases with their neighbouring text and repeated uses before accepting the catalog revision.

## Practical release checks

- Keep technical checks for catalog coverage, exact tokens, plurals and formatting. Report their result separately from language approval.
- Require review of every new or changed phrase in a shipped locale and its affected workflow. Give special attention to primary actions, prices, destructive actions, expiry and recovery instructions. Fix unresolved meaning errors and misleading actions before release.
- Check naturalness explicitly. A grammatical phrase with unfamiliar wording remains open until the reviewer accepts it or a documented terminology requirement explains it. Avoid repeated synonym changes based only on a model's preference.
- Confirm that rendered text, including real variable values and plurals, remains understandable and readable at supported sizes. Existing pseudolocalization and source checks cannot certify this.
- Record the exact locale, catalog revision, reviewer, reviewed states and remaining exceptions. If review was automated only, report it as automated review. This research has not reviewed or certified any translated catalog.

Suggested review instruction: "Preserve the meaning and task outcome. Prefer words ordinary users of this locale use in this context. Flag literal, formal, rare or awkward wording even if grammatical. Check approved terminology and consistent address. For each issue, give the message key, reason, context and a proposed correction. Mark uncertain choices for language review; do not invent a certainty score."

The FormatJS skill includes ICU/React-Intl-specific rules and sample code. Do not copy those mechanisms into i18next JSON-v4 or bypass Insignia's exact-money, plural/count and Preact contracts. Its instruction to preserve names, quantities, negation and action meaning transfers; syntax and formatting implementation must follow our own runtime. It explicitly separates proven errors from ambiguous terminology and acceptable synonyms, avoiding endless changes based on an agent's preferences.

The [i18next best-practices page](https://www.i18next.com/principles/best-practices) independently supports complete translatable sentences and avoiding interpolation of known grammatical fragments. Dynamic names and values still require interpolation and examples. This matters to Insignia's variables and app-generated action/resource labels: different genders or cases can make an English-style generic sentence unnatural even when all placeholders survive validation.

## Insignia-specific preparation before a new language ships

Prepare a versioned terminology/style/context packet in the repository. It should distinguish admin staff from storefront buyers and define concepts such as product view versus the View action, garment option values versus decoration size, Save versus Publish, and restore-default wording versus remove-language. Use current localized Shopify UI/help as evidence for Shopify-owned concepts. Do not automatically translate merchant product titles, supplied option values, placements, artwork filenames or other supplied content.

Choose the actual target language/region and form of address first. Use the language guide's conventions, while retaining Insignia's own voice and domain meanings. Review translations by complete workflow/state, not alphabetic JSON batches without context. Keep source revisions and accepted terminology so later work reviews changed messages and affected neighbours rather than retranslating everything.

Each locale's shipped catalog must be a baseline separate from sparse merchant overrides. When the baseline languages are implemented, their resolver/registry must supply the appropriate defaults. Merchant reset should remove an override and reveal that baseline, not overwrite or erase it. Today's English-only implementation does not establish that other language catalogs have been written or reviewed.

Suggested acceptance order: semantic/terminology review; target-language naturalness review; automated token/plural/coverage checks; real-language desktop/phone/error-state review; qualified target-language acceptance. Automated reviewers may be independent agents, but label the result AI-reviewed until a qualified language reviewer has checked it. Back-translation or a grammar check alone is not evidence that the original target wording sounds natural.

A small project-specific translation-review skill is a reasonable next artifact, combining these sources with Insignia's glossary and existing engineering checks. Its inputs should be exact locale, changed keys, context/screens and terminology; its output should identify concrete issues, uncertain choices and approved wording. That skill is proposed, not installed or created in this research task.
