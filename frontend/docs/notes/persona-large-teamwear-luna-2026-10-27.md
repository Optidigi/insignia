# Large teamwear catalogue walkthrough — 2026-10-27

## Scope and route

Walked the local Insignia preview as a Shopify merchant, starting at `/admin/home`, opening the existing T-shirt draft `15464675246464`, then the allowed `variant-many` fixture. Also opened its alignment-review fixture and used “Preview as customer.” I did not save or publish a product, and made no Shopify dev-store writes.

This is a synthetic UI walkthrough, not measured human usability research. The fixture is 24 colours with **4 variants per colour** (96 fixture variants), not the requested 8 sizes / 192 variants. Its colour labels are Black, White, Navy, and Sample colour 4–24. Three groups start with images; the other 21 say “Image needed.” The fixture’s image chooser has 16 product images, including Black, White, Navy front/back/side photos and shared Front/Back/side images. It does not contain 24 supplier photos or the requested three crop outliers.

## What I tried and what worked

1. The existing product draft opens on a 4-view, 6-placement editor. The default selected placement is Left chest. Its Printing and Embroidery methods are both enabled. This baseline product editor does not expose garment colour, garment size, or quantity controls.
2. The large-range fixture’s Front image panel is grouped by Colour. It presents one row per colour with a variant count, a preview, and Add/Change image. Since the fixture has more than eight groups, it also shows “Find variant images.” The image chooser has a product-image search and All / Unused / In use filters. It is straightforward to assign one image to a colour group, but I saw no automatic mapping from a colour-named variant photo to its matching group.
3. “Edit grouping” offers Same image for all variants, Colour, Size, and “Add another option.” Its helper says the grouping applies to every view of the product. The group list and image assignment are per view: Front and Back have separate image rows. For 24 unique colour photos, Colour is the useful grouping; it avoids making size-specific rows when all 8 sizes share the same photo. If each colour needs a distinct photo on both Front and Back, the visible workflow implies 24 image assignments per view (48 total); that count is an inference from the per-view UI, not a completed 24-photo run. Bulk “Choose image” / “Use shared image” appears after selecting groups, but one shared image for every colour would be wrong when each shirt colour has its own photo.
4. In the alignment-review fixture, Black is marked “Check alignment.” The canvas says “Check Black on Front” and offers “Confirm alignment.” Selecting a placement reveals “Shared artwork area” and “Adjust for Black”; that changes to “Alignment for Black” with “Use shared area.” Width, height and centre controls remain available. This is a real per-colour-group artwork-area override in the local preview, so the crop outliers can be handled individually. It is scoped to a colour group, view and selected placement; Front and Back artwork areas may need separate adjustments. “Confirm alignment” is a review acknowledgement, separate from changing the area. I explored the override, returned to the shared area and used Undo; Save draft was disabled afterward. No setup was saved.
5. “Preview as customer” opens a second local tab titled “Insignia | Product designer,” marked “Customer preview · Current draft.” The designer has a Product view selector (Front, Back, Left side, Right side) and Add your artwork. Choosing Front body and then Add artwork lets me add Back body separately. Each placement has its own artwork, method and print-size controls. In this seed Embroidery was selected by default; selecting Printing changed the shown amount from €30 to €5. A local demo artwork (`apps/web/test/admin/support/assets/order-demo/north-coast-chest-replacement.png`) was uploaded to Back and then reused from the Files list on Front. The same artwork appeared on both views without a second upload. This is a sample asset, not merchant artwork.

## Buyer handoff and limits

After adding the sample art to both placements, I scrolled the preview and re-read its full accessibility tree. It still offered only the placement cards and Add artwork. There was no Continue, Review, Shopify colour/garment-size selector, quantity field, cart, or checkout action in this **Current draft customer preview**. The print-size choice is decoration size, not T-shirt size. Therefore colour-photo switching, the requested 8 garment sizes, quantities, cart contents and fulfillment details are **not verifiable in this preview**. This does not establish that the public Shopify product page lacks those controls; the preview simply does not expose that part of the buyer flow.

For a Front+Back printing product, the sample buyer flow exposes a method choice at each placement, and this seed offers Embroidery as well as Printing. A merchant who wants printing only would need to turn off Embroidery for each placement (or accept that buyers choose between both methods) and review prices separately for both positions.

## Prioritized merchant judgment

1. **Buyer confidence is the main unverified handoff.** The admin handoff opens a designer preview but not the product’s Shopify colour/size/quantity/cart flow. A later integration must show the exact selected variant photo and garment size/quantity beside the customizer so a team buyer can verify each size run before ordering.
2. **Grouping is useful and the scope is mostly visible.** Colour grouping reduces the conceptual workload from 192 variant rows to 24 colour groups when sizes share their photo. The helper explicitly says grouping applies to every view, while each view has its own image list; making that distinction even clearer would help avoid missing Back assignments.
3. **Photo assignment still repeats by unique image.** Search is helpful for 24 groups, but distinct colour photos still require a per-colour choice for each view. Bulk assignment helps only when selected groups should share one image. A practical improvement would be a safe match action that proposes the Shopify variant image with the same colour label for each group, while leaving exceptions reviewable. It should not silently overwrite a colour-specific photo.
4. **The alignment tools fit the stated exception pattern.** The preview supports per-colour placement-area overrides and a “Use shared area” reset, so three outlier images do not call for a global alignment switch. The unit of work is per affected colour, view and print area; the merchant must visually check each affected combination and confirm review.
5. **Front and Back are separate buyer selections.** The buyer can add Front body and Back body independently and reuse one uploaded art file, which works for the same team logo on both sides. Method and price are also per placement, so a print-only range requires deliberate method configuration.

## Source boundary

Observed labels and behavior above are from the local synthetic preview. The colour grouping, bulk group assignment and per-group area controls I inspected live under `apps/web/test/admin/support/placement-variant-photo-panel.tsx` and `placement-workflow-preview.tsx`; this is preview/test-support UI, not proof of production support. In production `apps/web/src/islands/MerchantConfigEditor.tsx`, the source shows a shared-versus-individual variant selector and a way to use that variant’s Shopify image, with a 500-variant truncation notice. Do not treat the polished group workflow as integrated production behavior.
