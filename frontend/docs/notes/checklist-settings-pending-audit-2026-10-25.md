## Findings

### P3 · Medium confidence — separate the staff and app access labels

The settings-permissions-view-only pair shows a View only badge, then explains that the signed-in staff member can view product configurations and should ask an owner to review that staff account’s product-write access. The same section then says “Insignia app access: product editing available, publication settings available.” Both statements are accurate: the app has its Shopify grant, while the staff member’s account cannot edit. The existing paragraph already gives the right recovery step, so this is a copy polish rather than a recovery failure. Still, labeling the two facts as “Your Shopify staff access” and “Insignia app grant” would make their different scopes easier to scan and reduce a momentary apparent contradiction. Evidence: [desktop](/Users/pc/Development/Insignia/.artifacts/admin-popup-review-2026-10-24/settings/desktop/settings-permissions-view-only.png), [phone](/Users/pc/Development/Insignia/.artifacts/admin-popup-review-2026-10-24/settings/mobile/settings-permissions-view-only.png), [permission source](/Users/pc/Development/Insignia/insignia/apps/web/src/islands/ReadinessPermissions.tsx:209). Shopify’s Scopes API reports app scopes separately from staff authorization; the application source also checks them as distinct facts. See [Scopes API](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/app-home/latest/apis/authentication-and-data/scopes-api) and [Shopify authentication and authorization](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/authentication-authorization).

### Privacy export efficiency — not a finding

The per-order Download data action is the deliberate current minimum: each Insignia purchase is exported separately, and a row with no Insignia data offers no download. The sample has two requested orders but only one exportable order, so it does not establish burdensome volume. No request-level package or endpoint is implied by this audit. If actual requests commonly contain many exportable orders, revisit the effort with merchants while preserving the existing per-order authorization, availability, and retention checks. Evidence: [desktop detail](/Users/pc/Development/Insignia/.artifacts/admin-popup-review-2026-10-24/settings/desktop/settings-privacy-detail.png), [phone detail](/Users/pc/Development/Insignia/.artifacts/admin-popup-review-2026-10-24/settings/mobile/settings-privacy-detail.png), [export source](/Users/pc/Development/Insignia/insignia/apps/web/src/islands/MerchantPrivacy.tsx:467).

### Billing amount boundary — partial disclosure, no inferred net charge

The discounted sample shows the USD 49.99 base rate per 30 days, USD 5.00 off that rate, two discounted cycles remaining, an end date, and the current billing period. It does not show a next-charge date or net total. The current BillingView exposes provider recurring price and discount metadata but no verified next-charge amount/date; its type explicitly describes the discount as metadata, not a computed price. Keep those values separate. Do not subtract or otherwise manufacture an expected charge. If Shopify later supplies an exact upcoming charge and date, the UI could display those provider facts. Otherwise the Shopify management handoff is the source for plan selection and charge management. The current product-truth note records this deliberate boundary. See [discounted capture](/Users/pc/Development/Insignia/.artifacts/admin-popup-review-2026-10-24/settings/desktop/settings-billing-discounted.png), [BillingView](/Users/pc/Development/Insignia/insignia/apps/web/src/shared/billing-view.ts:1), and [billing decisions](/Users/pc/Development/Insignia/insignia/docs/delivery/notes/settings-product-truth-review-2026-10-25.md:33).
