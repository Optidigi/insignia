# Product placement setup: competitor workflows

First-party documentation review, 22 October 2026. These sources describe vendor workflows and constraints; they are not merchant interviews or proof that one layout is universally preferred.

## Strongest documented patterns

- **DecoNetwork treats a view as the image frame and areas as locations on that view.** Product setup offers supplier-provided views or merchant-defined views, with per-color images, one shared image, or recoloring templates. A view can contain several areas. At product level, methods can be selected by Smart Select rules or explicitly; each area can then have its own dimensions, position, shape, and compatible processes. Save-and-test in the Designer is part of the documented flow. This supports an overview organized by product view with its locations nested underneath. Pitfalls: a single shared image cannot show the chosen color; an area with no compatible process is hidden. [Configuring Product Views & Assigning Decoration Areas for Products](https://help.deconetwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/235607368-Configuring-Product-Views-Assigning-Decoration-Areas-for-Products)
- **InkSoft makes the merchant assemble a blank product across product tabs, while keeping setup product-local.** General setup carries sizes/weights, store/category availability, methods and print-price-grid assignments; Colors & Images attaches color images to sides; Pricing supports a fixed price or rule plus customer-price overrides for size upcharges; Print Regions names a customer-visible zone, defines physical width/height, and lets the merchant place and scale it on each side. Up to four regions are allowed per side in this workflow. The product-facing region name is a useful copy lesson: use terms shoppers understand. [Add New Product (Manually)](https://help.inksoft.com/hc/en-us/articles/46724094169371-Add-New-Product-Manually)
- **Zakeke is a product-personalizer layered over a store product.** The documented hierarchy is product → sides → print areas; the store image is a preview surface, and each area also defines the print-ready output. Store attributes feed a variant matrix; variants can be managed independently, but the store must own the underlying options. Zakeke also offers a distinct POD catalog source with provider fulfillment, which is a separate setup path from linking an existing store item. Its guide places method configuration for customer tools/output after the product's side and area setup, supporting a boundary between product geometry and personalization behavior. [Create a customizable product](https://zakeke.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360026668693-Create-a-customizable-product)
- **POD pricing reflects provider constraints, not a merchant-defined method matrix.** Printful's catalog price includes the blank and one print area; additional print placements and larger sizes (2XL+) can add cost, and base cost varies by fulfillment region. For a provider-backed product, show the provider's supported placements and cost effects as supplied constraints. Avoid presenting arbitrary merchant-editable techniques, geometry, or prices as if they change the fulfillment configuration. [How does Printful’s product pricing work?](https://help.printful.com/hc/en-us/articles/50262099109777-How-does-Printful-s-product-pricing-work)

## IA implications for Insignia

Use a product setup overview to show product/variant context, views, and the list of decoration locations with their setup state. A location row should reveal which methods are eligible there, without claiming the method is usable before geometry and price requirements are complete.

The focused location editor should keep the placement image and geometry beside that location’s allowed methods, decoration sizes, and prices. That follows DecoNetwork's area-to-process relationship and avoids the extra back-and-forth implied by managing geometry in one editor and commercial readiness in another. Keep shared method defaults reusable in the existing top-level Methods resource; product/location eligibility and final product prices belong in the product setup.

Treat color/size variants as explicit exceptions when the printable region or image differs. The source patterns suggest keeping size as a storefront variant unless physical print bounds differ; a changed production area may require a distinct location/file configuration. For POD-linked products, distinguish provider-managed availability and cost from merchant-defined product setup.

These references come from different systems: DecoNetwork and InkSoft are print-shop catalog/production workflows; Zakeke personalizes store products and can also connect a POD catalog; Printful owns provider-side technique and fulfillment costs. Their terminology and pricing behavior should not be copied wholesale into Insignia.
