## Material findings

1. **The expansion has breadth, but the synthesis still needs its new reasoning.** The inventory covers a much wider first-party roster and the operational comparison examines useful additional work models. Yet the synthesis's native-reference argument still names the original four apps, and its recommendations do not identify what changed after the expansion. Add a small evidence-to-decision comparison for Launch Check, Digital Products, Subscriptions, Order Printer and Knowledge Base. Counting apps is not the deliverable; showing which Home decisions they challenge is.

2. **Two evidence labels contradict the deeper comparison.** The inventory calls Subscriptions and Fraud Control documented work areas with no Home evidence. Shopify explicitly puts Subscriptions Performance on its app index and describes Apps > Fraud Control as opening its dashboard. Both qualify as documented Home/overview under this inventory's own definition. This does not prove their current visual layout. Sources: [Subscriptions analytics](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/purchase-options/subscriptions/shopify-subscriptions/analytics), [Fraud Control](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/fraud-prevention/fraud-control-app).

3. **The rankings sometimes confuse domain overlap with design-reference value.** Subscriptions is ranked low because Insignia entitlement is different, even though the operational note correctly studies customer contracts. Its useful lesson is a lifecycle resource with recoverable failures, not Insignia app billing. Order Printer is also low because it is an output tool, though it gives unusually relevant evidence for keeping production actions on order-owned pages. Preserve domain boundaries, but rate structural reference value separately. Conversely, Collabs' performance totals contribute less to today's Home decision than either example.

4. **Flow evidence needs the new successful observation.** The parent reports that the official `/apps/flow/activity` route now renders the no-runs state, and `/apps/flow` renders the first-use landing with templates/news. The operational note's opening statement that both routes remained blank is superseded. Preserve the earlier failure as history only if useful. A loading Select trigger editor still does not establish a populated editor, working workflow, or successful run.

## What the extra archetypes actually change

| Reference | Distinct lesson supported by the evidence | Concrete consequence to evaluate for Insignia |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Launch Check | Automatic assessment and merchant confirmation coexist. A rerun may carry manual edits while retaining the new original assessment. | A customer-test acknowledgment must remain distinguishable from current theme/configuration evidence. A later theme change can warrant a new check without rewriting what the merchant previously confirmed. Do not copy a broad Ready label or introduce an assessment service. |
| Digital Products | Products itself can be Home, with Orders elsewhere. | Explicitly compare a small work overview against improving the existing Products landing. The existence of several resource-first Shopify apps means a separate dashboard is a product choice that should earn its extra navigation and maintenance. | Subscriptions and Flow | Lifecycle status, execution status, and work requiring intervention are different concepts. | The proposed artwork task needs an actor and reason, not just a non-ready badge. ## Missing archetypes and limits

The roster is already broad enough. The meaningful omissions are in the comparison, rather than additional app names: assessment with manual confirmation, resource-as-Home, resource-owned execution, and lifecycle exceptions deserve equal treatment alongside onboarding and analytics. Inbox supplies a further human-handoff example, but its agent Overview and staff conversation queue are different documented contexts. Do not imply the Overview necessarily contains the actionable queue without native evidence. See [Inbox conversations](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/inbox/conversations).

Checklist Design's bundled Dashboard asks for relevant activity and attention items; Onboarding asks for a first action and transition; Notifications describes a chronological feed with read/unread states. A recent-order list is not evidence that Insignia needs unread notifications. Those checklists are coverage prompts, not proof of polished native composition. Shopify's [setup-guide composition](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/app-home/v2.0/patterns/compositions/setup-guide) supports completed-task tracking, so omitting a progress count remains an Insignia scope/evidence decision.

The existing state matrix is substantially stronger than generic dashboard advice. Its most valuable safeguards are bounded recent orders, independent panel failures, permission-aware content and retention-aware artwork reasons. The next research synthesis should make those choices visibly traceable to the expanded references, then reserve polish and hierarchy acceptance for an actual rendered Home study. No backend or app implementation is needed to finish this research.
